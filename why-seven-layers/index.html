Why us

Problems live
at the boundaries.

Most technology failures don't happen inside a layer. They happen where two layers meet — and where two suppliers each insist the fault is on the other side. Working the whole stack is not a sales position. It is the thing that removes that argument.

The argument

Four suppliers, three boundaries, nobody responsible.

A typical estate has one company for hardware, another for network, another for cloud, and someone else entirely for the application. Each is competent. The gaps between them are where outages live, and every gap is a conversation about whose fault it is.

Questions

The things people ask first.

Why does working across seven layers matter?+

Most technology failures happen at a boundary — between the network and the platform, or the application and the infrastructure beneath it. When separate suppliers own each side, that boundary is where responsibility gets disputed instead of fixed. One partner across the stack removes the argument.

Do we have to engage across all seven layers?+

No, and most clients don't. Nearly every engagement starts at one layer. The point is that when the problem turns out to sit a layer below where you thought, we can follow it rather than hand you back a ticket.

How do you price work?+

Scope first, then a fixed price agreed in writing before anything starts. Ongoing operational work is monthly with no long-term lock-in. If scope changes mid-project we re-quote rather than quietly billing more.

What happens when a project finishes?+

Documentation and handover, then thirty days of support included. After that you can take a care plan, bring it in-house, or do neither — all three are fine, and we will not make the third one difficult.
