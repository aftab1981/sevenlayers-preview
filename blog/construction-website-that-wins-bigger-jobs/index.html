The construction website that wins bigger jobs

15 August 2026 · 6 min read

Most construction and trade businesses win small jobs on word of mouth and lose bigger ones to firms that look more established. The gap is rarely capability. It is evidence — and a website is where evidence lives now, because it is where the buyer checks you before they call.

The project gallery is the whole website

Everything else is supporting material. Someone deciding whether you can handle a £60,000 job wants to see a £60,000 job you have already done.

Make each project a page, not a photograph in a grid:

  • Before, during, after. The during shots are what separate you from a firm that bought stock images.
  • What the job actually was — scope, duration, and any complication you solved. The complication is the persuasive part.
  • Where it was, at least at town level. Local proof beats national proof for local work.
  • What the client wanted and got. Two sentences is plenty.

Ten projects documented properly beat sixty photographs in a lightbox.

What bigger buyers check that smaller ones don't

When the job is large enough to involve a procurement process, a facilities manager or an architect, the checklist changes. Put these where they can be found in one click:

  • Insurance — public liability and employer's liability, with cover levels.
  • Accreditations and trade memberships you genuinely hold. Only those you hold.
  • Health and safety approach, in a paragraph rather than a policy PDF.
  • Company registration and VAT details, where they apply.
  • References you can share on request.

None of this is exciting, and all of it removes a reason to eliminate you from a shortlist.

Make the quote request easy and specific

“Contact us” is a wasted opportunity. A short form that asks the two or three things you always end up asking anyway — type of work, rough timescale, location — filters the enquiries and makes the first call shorter. Add a photo upload. People will happily send you a picture of the problem, and it is worth more than a paragraph describing it.

The mobile reality

Your buyer is often standing on a site, in daylight, one-handed, on a mediocre connection. Big tap targets, a phone number that dials in one tap, and a page that loads before they give up. Anything clever that only works on a desktop is not working.

Get found locally

Most construction work is geographic. A page per service and a page per area you genuinely cover — not fifty thin pages for towns you have never worked in, which search engines have been discounting for years. A handful of honest, specific pages outperforms a directory of invented ones. Our guide to local SEO covers the mechanics.

What to fix first

  1. Document five real projects properly.
  2. Put insurance and accreditations one click from the homepage.
  3. Replace “Contact us” with a three-field quote request and a photo upload.
  4. Check the whole thing on a phone, outdoors.
